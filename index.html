Safe Decentralized Multi-Agent Control using Black-Box Predictors, Conformal Decision Policies, and Control Barrier Functions

Sacha Huriot, Hussein Sibai
Washington University in St. Louis
Article Accepted for ICRA 2025
Paper Code arXiv

Abstract

We address the challenge of safe control in decentralized multi-agent robotic settings, where agents use uncertain black-box models to predict other agents’ trajectories. We use the recently proposed conformal decision theory to adapt the restrictiveness of control barrier functions-based safety constraints based on observed prediction errors. We use these constraints to synthesize controllers that balance between the objectives of safety and task accomplishment, despite the prediction errors. We provide an upper bound on the average over time of the value of a monotonic function of the difference between the safety constraint based on the predicted trajectories and the constraint based on the ground truth ones. We validate our theory through experimental results showing the performance of our controllers when navigating a robot in the multi-agent scenes in the Stanford Drone Dataset

Stanford Drone Dataset Experiments

We implemented the conformal update in Python on top of the code used in Conformal Decision Theory. SDD is a large dataset of annotated 30 fps birds-eye-view videos of various types of agents that navigate in different high traffic areas of the campus of Stanford University. We used the trajectory predictor darts which, for every scenario, frame, and pedestrian agent on that frame, predicts the positions of the agents for the next 40 frames. A virtual robot is simulated in the scene, equipped with the darts predictor, and has to reach a specific goal position while avoiding the real positions of surrounding dynamic agents. In our experiments, we use a CBF-based controller instead of a model predictive one to achieve safety. We assume that our robot has double integrator dynamics and use the artificial potential fields-based CBF from for collision avoidance.

There are several parameters we experimented with to evaluate the performance of our conformal controller. They can be divided into two groups: the controller’s inherent parameters (the sensor’s sampling time and the CBF's parameters), and the learning parameters (the learning rate and the target loss). In order to assess the controller’s performance, we used several key metrics: the time for reaching the goal, the number of frames at which the agent was at collision, the minimal distance between the ego agent and the pedestrians, and finally the average loss of our conformal controller. Our approach does not guarantee collision avoidance, but a bound on the average loss that is a surrogate for the average number of CBF constraints violations.
